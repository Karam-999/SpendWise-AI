# Go-To-Market Strategy

## Target User

**Primary persona:** Engineering Manager or VP of Engineering at a 10–50 person B2B SaaS startup, Series A or bootstrapped, spending $2,000–$15,000/month across 3–6 AI tools. They manage the eng budget, approved tool purchases over the last 12 months during the AI hype cycle, and now face board pressure to show unit economics. They've never audited their AI tool spend — they just approved each tool individually and have no aggregate view.

**Secondary persona:** Solo founder or CTO at a 2–8 person team running Cursor + Claude + ChatGPT and paying ~$200–$500/month total. Not enough to hire a finance person, but enough to feel the burn when margins are tight. They'd use SpendWise out of curiosity and share the results on Twitter if the savings are meaningful.

## What They Google Right Before Wanting This Tool

- "cursor vs copilot pricing comparison 2026"
- "how to reduce AI tool costs startup"
- "claude team vs pro which plan"
- "openai api costs too high alternatives"
- "AI tool budget template startup"

They're comparison-shopping or reacting to a surprisingly high invoice. They're not looking for an audit tool — they don't know one exists. That's the wedge: we intercept them at the comparison-shopping moment and offer something better than a blog post.

## First 100 Users in 30 Days — $0 Budget

**Week 1: Launch on Hacker News**
Post "Show HN: I built a free AI spend audit tool — found $840/mo in waste on my own stack." HN rewards tools that are genuinely free, solve a real problem, and have clean UX. Target: 50+ audits completed from the HN post alone.

**Week 1–2: Direct outreach on X/Twitter**
DM 40–50 founders who've recently tweeted about AI tool costs or posted their stack. Message: "Hey — I built a free audit tool that checks your AI stack for overspend. Want to try it? Takes 60 seconds." Not selling anything — just offering a free tool. Target: 15 users from DMs.

**Week 2: Reddit distribution**
Post in r/startups ("I audited 10 startup AI stacks — here's what I found") with real anonymised data from the first batch of audits. Include shareable URLs. No self-promotion — just useful data with a link. Target: 20 users from Reddit.

**Week 2–3: Indie Hackers and Dev.to**
Write a detailed post: "How much are you overpaying for AI tools? I checked." Include a table of common waste patterns (Team plans for solo users, duplicate coding tools, etc.) and link to the tool. Target: 10 users.

**Week 3–4: Shareable URL viral loop**
Every audit generates a public URL with OG tags. Users who find real savings share on Slack/Twitter ("I could save $X/mo on AI tools"). Each share is a free impression. Target: 5 organic signups per day from shares.

**Total target: 100+ users by Day 30.**

## The Unfair Distribution Channel

Credex already has a customer list of companies that have *purchased* discounted AI credits. These are companies with proven AI spend who actively seek savings. Emailing them: "We built a free audit tool — see exactly where your AI stack could be optimised" is a warm outreach to pre-qualified leads with a free offer. No cold email tool needed. This channel is unavailable to any competitor building a similar tool — it's Credex's unique asset.
